# Supplement Tier List — Denis

Evidence-based tiering of Denis's supplement stack, scored against training priorities:
gravel cycling > strength/hypertrophy > calisthenics > mobility/durability > recovery/fatigue > keto nutrition.

Tiers reflect **strength of evidence × relevance to goals**, not just "does it work in general."

- **Tier 1** — Proven, high-impact. Keep as-is.
- **Tier 2** — Strong for a specific goal or corrects a likely deficiency.
- **Tier 3** — Promising but modest / mixed evidence.
- **Tier 4** — Micronutrient insurance or low-value; fine but not a priority.
- **Skip** — Redundant given diet; no meaningful benefit.

Current stack source: `people/denis/nutrition.md`. General information, not medical advice.

---

## Tier 1 — Proven, high-impact

| Supplement | Dose | Evidence | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 10 g | Among the most robustly studied ergogenics: strength, power, lean mass, repeat-sprint capacity, plus cognitive/recovery benefits. Extra valuable on keto (low dietary creatine). | Strength, hypertrophy, gravel surges |
| **Caffeine** | 200 mg | Strong, consistent ergogenic — endurance time-to-exhaustion, power, RPE, focus. ~3 mg/kg pre-effort is the studied sweet spot. | Gravel + gym performance |
| **Electrolytes (Na/K blend)** | NaCl 70% + KCl 30%, iodized | Keto raises renal sodium/potassium loss (lower insulin). Adequate sodium prevents fatigue, cramps, poor endurance — highest-leverage keto add. | Endurance, cramp prevention |
| **Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)** | 2100 mg | Strong cardiovascular/anti-inflammatory evidence; emerging support for muscle protein synthesis, recovery, reduced soreness. | Recovery, long-term health |

## Tier 2 — Strong for a specific goal or corrects a likely deficiency

| Supplement | Dose | Evidence | Flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beta-alanine** | 6 g | Good evidence for 1–4 min high-intensity efforts (VO2/threshold intervals) via muscle carnosine buffering. Benefit is chronic (needs weeks of loading), not acute. | Split doses to limit paresthesia |
| **Magnesium (glycinate + malate)** | ~680 mg combined | Deficiency common, worsened by keto diuresis; supports neuromuscular function, sleep, recovery. Glycinate is well-absorbed. | Confirm figures are *elemental* Mg, not compound weight; RDA ~420 mg. GI upset if too high |
| **D3 + K2** | 8000 IU D3 + 200 µg K2 | Corrects very common vitamin D insufficiency; supports bone, immune, muscle. K2 directs calcium to bone, not arteries. | 8000 IU is high for daily long-term use — get a 25-OH-D blood test to avoid over-supplementing |

## Tier 3 — Promising but modest / mixed evidence

| Supplement | Dose | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collagen + vitamin C** | 8,700 mg + C | Growing (not definitive) evidence that ~15 g collagen + vitamin C ~30–60 min before loading supports tendon/ligament collagen synthesis. | Pre-training timing (as dosed) is correct. Relevant to calisthenics joint durability |
| **Citrulline malate** | 6 g | Mixed — some studies show more reps-to-failure and reduced soreness via NO/blood flow; others null. Low downside, modest upside. | Keep if tolerated |
| **Ashwagandha** *(optional trial)* | 300–600 mg standardized (KSM-66/Sensoril) | Best evidence for lower cortisol/perceived stress and better sleep. Strength/VO2max signals are small and often industry-funded; testosterone effect weak in trained men. | Recovery/fatigue tool, not an ergogenic. **Can raise thyroid hormone** — check first if any thyroid issue. Rare hepatotoxicity; don't mix with sedatives. Cycle 6–8 wks on / break |

## Tier 4 — Micronutrient insurance (fine, watch ceilings)

| Supplement | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Zinc + copper + B6** | 45 mg Zn / 4 mg Cu / 6.3 mg B6 | Immune/hormone support if deficient. **45 mg zinc exceeds the 40 mg upper limit** — copper offsets depletion risk, but don't push zinc higher |
| **Brazil nuts (selenium)** | 2 nuts | Covers selenium (thyroid, antioxidant). Don't exceed ~3–4/day — selenium has a narrow safe range |
| **B1 (benfotiamine)** *(optional)* | RDA-level | Fat-soluble thiamine. Most research is in diabetic neuropathy, not athletes. Keto diuresis mildly depletes water-soluble Bs, and carb-fueling days raise thiamine demand — but plain thiamine covers that cheaper. Harmless insurance, no performance upside |

## Skip — redundant or no meaningful benefit

| Supplement | Reason |
|---|---|
| **B5 (pantothenic acid)** | Ubiquitous in diet (eggs, meat, dairy, seeds — all staples here). Deficiency essentially nonexistent; no ergogenic/recovery benefit when not deficient |

## Priority flags

1. **Dose ceilings to monitor:** D3 (8000 IU) and zinc (45 mg) are the two long-term risks — a 25-OH-D blood test and keeping zinc ≤40 mg de-risks both.
2. **Magnesium total:** confirm glycinate + malate labels report *elemental* magnesium before assuming ~680 mg real intake.
3. **Ashwagandha × thyroid:** already supporting thyroid via selenium + iodine; ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone, so verify if any thyroid condition.
4. **No fatigue risk from the stack itself** — it's well-built for keto endurance + strength. Gaps are dosing precision, not missing ingredients. Spend attention/cost budget on nailing Tier 1–2 dosing before adding Tier 3–4 items.
